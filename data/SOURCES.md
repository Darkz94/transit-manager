# Stage 1 map data sources

## Province boundaries

- **Source:** Natural Earth Admin 1 States and Provinces, generalized provincial polygons.
- **License:** Public domain. Natural Earth states its vector and raster data may be used, modified, and distributed for any purpose.
- **Data page:** https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/10m-admin-1-states-provinces/
- **Terms:** https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/
- **Credits:** Made with Natural Earth.
- **Project transformation:** Filtered to South Africa's nine provinces, simplified for a small offline game map, and normalized the historical source label "Orange Free State" to "Free State".

## Municipality anchor points

- **Source:** South Africa Administrative Divisions dataset, Open Admin Data, last updated 1 June 2026.
- **License:** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0).
- **Source repository:** https://github.com/open-admin-data/south-africa-administrative-divisions
- **License text:** https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
- **Attribution:** South Africa Administrative Divisions Dataset (CC BY 4.0), Open Admin Data.
- **Project transformation:** Retained municipality id, English and local names, parent district, province, and coordinates; omitted unrelated source fields; normalized the source spelling "Nothern Cape" to "Northern Cape".
- **Data scope:** 213 municipality anchor points. These are administrative place anchors, not a complete settlement, suburb, or street catalogue.

## National road route references and schematic corridors

The project route list follows the route numbers shown on the South African Department of Transport's 2023 Transport Statistics Bulletin national road network map: N1–N12 (excluding N13), N14, N17, and N18. SANRAL's interactive network map is also a reference for later route geometry checks.

- **Department of Transport source:** Transport Statistics Bulletin 2023, Map A 1: National Road Network: https://www.transport.gov.za/documents/11623/0/Transport+Statistics+Bulletin+2023.pdf
- **SANRAL reference map:** https://www.nra.co.za/interactive-map/
- **Project transformation:** The data file contains project-curated, hand-drawn anchor corridors for the 15 route numbers above. These are visual map lines, not extracted or digitized official road centerlines.

The route strokes are schematic planning corridors. Stage 4 currently uses only the N1–N4 points as a lightweight prototype graph for route finding and truck animation. The additional national routes are map-display only. None are surveyed road centerlines, turn-by-turn routes, or a complete road graph; derived route distances are provisional and must not be presented as authoritative real-world distances.

### Candidate for usable offline road geometry

**Recommendation:** use Geofabrik's South Africa OpenStreetMap `.osm.pbf` extract as the source for a locally processed road graph, subject to an ODbL distribution review and a size/performance prototype. The official download page listed `south-africa-latest.osm.pbf` at 404 MB, containing OSM data through 2026-11-09 21:22 UTC, when checked on 2026-11-11. This is a changing source snapshot, not a version to fetch silently during game runtime.

- **Candidate country extract:** https://download.geofabrik.de/africa/south-africa.html
- **OpenStreetMap attribution and license:** https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright
- **Extract format:** Geofabrik provides PBF for processing with tools such as Osmium; its page also lists Shapefile and GeoPackage packages. The current PBF is a source input only and should not be bundled unchanged in the Android game.
- **Prototype tooling:** GDAL/OGR exports the Greater Cape Town road features from the country PBF; `tools/build_osm_road_graph.py` clips them and reads original way refs and node coordinates directly from the PBF. The builder retains motorway through tertiary, unclassified, residential, living-street, and service classes and link roads, route refs/names, one-way tags, access and truck-restriction tags, bridge/tunnel/layer tags, segment distances, and shape coordinates. No edge is removed just because one access tag says `no` or `private`; those tags are retained so routing can apply restrictions for the selected game vehicle. With GDAL/OGR installed, run `ogr2ogr --config OGR_INTERLEAVED_READING YES -f GeoJSON build/cape-town-roads-source-service.geojson build/south-africa-latest.osm.pbf lines -spat 18 -34.5 19.3 -33.3 -where "highway IN ('motorway','motorway_link','trunk','trunk_link','primary','primary_link','secondary','secondary_link','tertiary','tertiary_link','unclassified','residential','living_street','service')" -lco RFC7946=YES`, then `python3 tools/build_osm_road_graph.py build/south-africa-latest.osm.pbf build/cape-town-roads-source-service.geojson build/cape-town-roads-with-service.json --source-snapshot 2026-11-09T21:22:17Z`. GDAL/OGR selects regional way IDs and tags; the builder retrieves their original node references and coordinates from the PBF, preserving OSM's shared-node topology. The result remains a candidate and needs directed route, connectivity, restriction, plausibility, size, and phone performance review before use. Keep the raw extract, exported GeoJSON, and generated candidate under ignored `build/`; none belongs in the Android app or repository by default.
- **Arterial-only variant:** for the smaller graph candidate, use the same `ogr2ogr` command but remove `,'unclassified','residential','living_street','service'` from the highway filter and write `build/cape-town-roads-arterials-source.geojson`; pass that file to the graph builder and choose a matching output name.
- **Current truck-aware source graph:** including `highway=service` in the Greater Cape Town extract produces 221,098 nodes and 158,698 edges (79.07 MiB JSON; 14.95 MiB gzip). Filtering to HGV-eligible roads leaves 140,605 edges and 196,954 active nodes; the largest weak component is 36,306 nodes (18.4%). This is a source candidate and is not suitable for direct game packaging.
- **Road-class comparison:** the HGV `arterial_network` has 23,816 eligible edges / 711 components / a 19,420-node largest component (88.5% of 21,942 active nodes). The motorway-through-secondary `major_truck_routes` has 15,952 eligible edges / 298 components / a 14,135-node largest component (94.4% of 14,979 active nodes). All twelve anchor points snap to the terminal's largest component within 0.5 km in each preset; the farthest snap is 0.41 km for arterial and 0.45 km for major routes. District route lengths are 0.8–29.6 km for arterial and 0.8–30.1 km for major routes.
- **Anchor-focused delivery candidate:** `tools/build_osm_delivery_candidate.py` retains the motorway-through-tertiary network plus residential, living-street, unclassified, and service edges within a configurable radius of the Cape Town terminal and 11 district anchors. At 0.5 km it contains 26,122 edges (2.20 MiB gzip); the largest weak component has 19,985 of 24,638 active HGV nodes (81.1%). All 12 points route from the terminal at a 0.5 km snap; 11/12 route at 0.2 km, with Hout Bay outside the main component at that tolerance. All six current vehicle/cargo category probes route all 12 points at the 0.5 km snap; the farthest snap is 0.19 km for vans and 0.41 km for HGV categories. District route lengths are 0.8–29.6 km. These are candidate-network distances and anchor snaps, not delivery-address access.
- **Last-mile limitation:** the 0.5 km-radius candidate improves snapping for several anchors compared with arterial roads alone, but Hout Bay still needs a closer main-component access edge or a reviewed anchor adjustment. Adding larger local-road buffers increased graph size and disconnected fragments without solving Hout Bay at 0.2 km. OSM service roads may be private or locally restricted; the preserved access tags must be checked before use: https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Service_road
- **Truck-profile probes:** the QA tool can filter explicit generic access, goods/HGV, and generic hazmat restrictions for game categories `van`, `rigid`, `reefer`, `prime_mover`, `safechem_rigid`, and `capebulk_tipper`. All game vehicles are treated as goods vehicles; HGV-specific restrictions apply to every fleet class except the vans. Probe profiles are category assumptions that match the current fleet; the fleet has no modeled dimensions or loaded gross weights. All profile routes remain candidate-network QA, not legal routing or real-world distance estimates.
- **Truck restriction handling:** the graph preserves generic access, vehicle, motor vehicle, goods, HGV, conditional access, hazmat, maximum weight/height/width/axle load/length, surface, smoothness, and speed tags where present. The QA tool applies a small, documented access subset by vehicle/cargo category. Conditional restrictions and numeric limits are counted and reported but are not evaluated because the game has no measured/design truck dimensions or weights yet. Turn restrictions and local destination access exceptions are also outside this prototype. Game profile labels do not assert statutory vehicle classes. OSM tagging is community data with varying coverage and semantics; do not treat this as complete South African legal routing.
- **QA commands:** create the anchor-focused dataset with `python3 tools/build_osm_delivery_candidate.py build/cape-town-roads-with-service.json build/cape-town-delivery-service-0p5km.json data/stage1_metro_districts.json data/stage1_terminals.json --local-access-radius-km 0.5`. Then run `python3 tools/evaluate_osm_road_graph.py build/cape-town-delivery-service-0p5km.json data/stage1_metro_districts.json data/stage1_terminals.json --vehicle-id rigid --cargo-id dry --road-class-set all --snap-radius-km 0.5 --report build/cape-town-delivery-service-rigid-qa.json`. Compare `--road-class-set all`, `arterial_network`, and `major_truck_routes` on the full source graph. Supported vehicle and cargo IDs mirror the game definitions in `scripts/operations.gd`. The report is not a gameplay route or real-world distance source.
- **Proposed production processing:** retain motorway, trunk, primary, secondary, and their connector classes for the national graph, then add a limited tertiary/local-road selection around playable metro districts only if the regional prototype shows it is useful. Keep map-drawing simplification separate from routing geometry.
- **Packaging gate:** prototype one playable region first, measure the resulting compressed Android asset size, load time, memory use, graph connectivity, and route-distance plausibility on a phone, then decide whether to package the national graph. Do not put the 404 MB source extract directly in the app.
- **License gate:** OSM data is ODbL 1.0. Provide visible in-game attribution and a license notice; review share-alike obligations for distributing a derived database and make the corresponding source data available as required. Keep the downloaded extract's date, processing script, filters, and attribution with each generated dataset. This note records a project recommendation, not a legal determination.
- **Project implementation:** Stage 4 still uses only the N1–N4 entries in `stage1_corridors.json`. Those schematic points must not feed route distance or travel time after the graph is replaced. Production routing distances and costs should come from a reviewed road graph, independently of map drawing simplification.

## Stage 1 prototype layers

- **Major city and town pins:** 40 manually curated seed coordinates for a first map pass. These points are approximate visual anchors and must be checked against the selected road dataset before being used to calculate travel distance or delivery time.
- **Metro district demo:** `stage1_metro_districts.json` contains 35 project-curated approximate anchor coordinates across Cape Town, Johannesburg, Pretoria, Durban, Gqeberha, Bloemfontein, and East London. `stage1_settlements.json` also contains Soweto and Centurion district anchors. These are interface placeholders for zoom and selection, not a complete or surveyed suburb-boundary dataset.
- **National route strokes:** schematic centerlines for N1–N12 (excluding N13), N14, N17, and N18. Their purpose is to show a broader national network and map hierarchy; they are not authoritative road geometry or routing paths.


## Terminal demonstration points

- **Source:** Project-created sample locations for the Stage 1 interface.
- **Data file:** `data/stage1_terminals.json`.
- **Scope:** Ten fictional, unowned demo terminal pins near Cape Town, Kimberley, Gqeberha, Bloemfontein, Durban, Mahikeng, Johannesburg, Pretoria, Polokwane, and Mbombela. The coordinates reuse sample city anchors or approximate city-center coordinates for visual placement.
- **Use limits:** These are interface placeholders, not existing facilities, operating rights, port access, or surveyed terminal sites. Stage 4 uses their city-center coordinates as provisional endpoints for the schematic N1–N4 route graph; resulting distances are game estimates, not authoritative road distances.
